4. EMPIRICAL SCALING - how the bias ACTUALLY responds to a change in f*B
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F-16's 1.33 m configuration sweep contains THREE pairs that differ ONLY by mono resolution,
i.e. three independent doublings of f*B on the same subject in one continuous hold. All
three are used; picking only the favourable one would misstate the result.

comparison (f*B x2)                 dz before   dz after     ratio
baseline -> mono800                      89.0       95.0     1.067
sub3 -> mono800_sub3                     82.2       65.0     0.791
sub3_rgb800 -> best                      97.0       86.0     0.887

   mean ratio 0.915   median 0.887   spread 0.115

   A pure PIXEL-domain bias scales away with f*B            -> ratio 0.50 per doubling
   A pure DEPTH-domain bias (background blending) does not  -> ratio 1.00 per doubling
   MEASURED                                                 -> ratio 0.91 per doubling

   Doubling f*B moves the bias by -9 % on average, with one of the three comparisons
   moving the WRONG WAY (+6.7 %). That is indistinguishable from no effect, and nowhere
   near the -50 % a fixed pixel offset would give. Quantisation fell 2x in every one of
   these pairs, so f*B certainly changed - the BIAS simply does not follow it.

   CAVEAT carried into the report: these doublings come from RESOLUTION, not BASELINE. They
   are the closest available probe of how the bias responds to f*B, not a substitute for
   measuring a wider baseline.

6. THE COUPLING THE BRIEF ASKS FOR (section 12): FOV sets the distance, distance sets B
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A candidate cannot be judged on baseline alone - a narrower lens pushes the full-body
distance out, and the required baseline grows as Z^2.

V-FOV deg       Z_min m    dz @Z_min     yaw @75 mm    B for <=5 deg
50.0               1.88        124.4          20.48            320.2
60.0               1.52         81.2          13.70            208.9
70.2               1.24         54.8           9.34            141.0
80.0               1.04         38.4           6.58             98.9
95.0               0.80         22.7           3.90             58.5

   Closed form:  B_req = H_body^2 * dd / (2 * h_px * W * tan(eps) * tan(vfov/2))
   Widening the LENS is as effective as widening the BASELINE, and cheaper: it lets the
   subject stand closer for the same full-body framing, and the requirement falls as Z^2.
